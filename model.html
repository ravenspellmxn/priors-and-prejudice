Block Belief Machine

A simulated mouse watches a drifting grating each trial and licks left or right. It has to learn two things at once: which way this grating moves, and which kind of block it is living in. Move the world's knobs and the observer's knobs separately and watch both beliefs move.

What the mouse saw stimulus × contrast, and the noisy observation

Filled dot = the truth, grating moved right Filled dot = the truth, grating moved left Hollow ring = what the mouse saw, m

How to read it. Each column is one trial. The dot's height is the contrast, how strong the motion was. The ring is the dot plus noise, and the thin line between them is the noise. Above the zero line looks like right, below looks like left. The mouse only ever gets the ring.

What's my env block belief after each trial

P(I am in an R-biased block) P(I am in an unbiased block) P(I am in an L-biased block) faint = this one sample, solid = mean of 500

How to read it. Three lines, one per kind of block the mouse might be in. Height is how sure the mouse is after that trial, from 0% to 100%, and the three always add up to 100%. Trial 0 is before any evidence, all three at 33%. The line that climbs is the block it believes in. Crossing the 90% line is "sure enough," and the tile at the top counts how many trials that took.

What's the current direction P(right) before and after looking

Gray = prior π, P(right) before looking, straight from the block belief Teal = posterior, P(right) after seeing m

How to read it. Both lines answer "how likely is it that this trial's grating moved right." Gray is the expectation before looking, which is just the block belief from the graph above turned into a lean. Teal is after seeing m. Above 50% the mouse licks right, below 50% it licks left. Teal hugging gray means the mouse is trusting the block; teal jumping away from gray means the observation won.

Mean accuracy at each trial, 500 runs

How to read it. Averaged over 500 runs: the share of runs where the lick on that trial was correct. Chance is 50%. It climbs as the block belief sharpens and the prior starts helping on faint trials.

Ideal-observer model by Alyssa Romportl (Eissa and Felsen labs, University of Colorado Anschutz), built as an interactive by Raven Spellman for the ABRCMS 2026 poster Priors and Prejudice. A simulation for building intuition, no animal data involved. The five steps, per trial:
  1. Block belief before the trial (flat 1/3 each on trial 1, then last trial's posterior).
  2. Prior for right: π = β·P(R-biased) + 0.5·P(unbiased) + (1 − β)·P(L-biased).
  3. Observation m drawn from Normal(stimulus × contrast, ζ²). Contrast is either the fixed level or drawn from the lab table (0, 0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 1 with probabilities 0.1, 0.3, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1).
  4. Likelihood of right = Φ(μ* / σ*) with μ* = ζ−2m / (σ−2 + ζ−2) and σ* = (σ−2 + ζ−2)−1/2; posterior by Bayes' rule with π.
  5. Lick the side with the larger posterior (LLR sign). Then, after feedback, update the block belief with the true stimulus using β, 0.5, 1 − β.
