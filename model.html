Observer inference side

0.80
Block likelihoods: P(right | R-biased) = β, P(right | unbiased) = 0.5, P(right | L-biased) = 1 − β. Block prior starts flat at 1/3 each.
0.020
Before each trial the observer keeps 1 − h of each block belief and spreads h over the other two blocks (predict_block). With h = 0 it can get stuck after a reversal. The task's blocks average about 50 trials, so h = 1/50.
0.02
With this chance the lick is a coin flip, whatever the mouse believes (apply_lapse). Real mice do this; it keeps accuracy below 100%.
The observer shares the world's ζ and uses σ = 0.49, the spread of the nine signed contrast levels, as its contrast prior. Both fixed.

How to read it. Each column is one trial. The dot's height is the contrast, how strong the motion was. The ring is the dot plus noise, and the thin line between them is the noise. Above the zero line looks like right, below looks like left. The mouse only ever gets the ring.

What's my env block belief after each trial

P(I am in an R-biased block) P(I am in an unbiased block) P(I am in an L-biased block) faint = this one sample, solid = mean of 500

How to read it. Three lines, one per kind of block the mouse might be in. Height is how sure the mouse is after that trial, from 0% to 100%, and the three always add up to 100%. Trial 0 is before any evidence, all three at 33%. The line that climbs is the block it believes in. The dashed 90% line is a reference. The tile at the top counts trials until the prior settles (within 0.05 of where it ends up, for 5 trials in a row).

What's the current direction P(right) before and after looking

Gray = prior π, P(right) before looking, straight from the block belief Blue = posterior, P(right) after seeing m

How to read it. Both lines answer "how likely is it that this trial's grating moved right." Gray is the expectation before looking, which is just the block belief from the graph above turned into a lean. Blue is after seeing m. Above 50% the mouse licks right, below 50% it licks left. Blue hugging gray means the mouse is trusting the block; blue jumping away from gray means the observation won.

The Bayesian observer from the poster (model from the Eissa and Felsen labs). A toy simulation for building intuition, no lab data involved. The steps, per trial:
  1. Block belief before the trial (flat 1/3 each on trial 1, then last trial's posterior).
  2. Prior for right: π = β·P(R-biased) + 0.5·P(unbiased) + (1 − β)·P(L-biased).
  3. Observation m drawn from Normal(stimulus × contrast, ζ²). Contrast is either the fixed level or drawn from the task mix (0, 0.0625, 0.125, 0.25, 1 with probabilities 0.1, 0.3, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1).
  4. Likelihood of right = Φ(μ* / σ*) with μ* = ζ−2m / (σ−2 + ζ−2) and σ* = (σ−2 + ζ−2)−1/2; posterior by Bayes' rule with π.
  5. Lick the side with the larger posterior (LLR sign). Then, after feedback, update the block belief with the true stimulus using β, 0.5, 1 − β. A small share of licks (the lapse rate) are random.
  6. Before the next trial, allow for a switch: keep 1 − h of each block belief and spread h over the other two. Learned = the prior π stays within 0.05 of where it settles for that block for 5 trials in a row.
